Lately, I've come to the realisation that almost everything I interact with is getting worse.
Not to an egregious level, but enough to cause pain and annoyance.

Here are some examples
* Amazon Prime serving ads over content that I've already paid for
* Spotify continuing to raise prices despite the app continuing to get slower
* My iPhone getting slower with each update and the battery draining faster
* New clothes (from mid-market stores) having pulled threads and seems tearing
* Furniture pilot holes not drilled correctly and wood arriving damaged
* Going into a shop and an employee tell me that I need to order it online as they don't stock it

I could go on. Perhaps you've had similar or even worse experiences.

This is enshittification.

In the discourse around this topic however, most of the focus goes to the technology that we interact with. But I've found this same phenomenon happening wherever I turn.

The question is - what is the cure?

The honest answer is there isn't one (at least within my control). The solution though is alternatives.

For clothing for example, I try to thrift all clothing. This is made incredibly easy with platforms like Vinted or Depop. Both of which will inevitably enshittify but for now, work well.

The technology sphere is where things get more interesting.
Mostly I need a way to play music, watch tv and movies, shop and a mobile computer of some description. There are of course more services such as PDF editing, file storage and video calls - but I didn't want to bite off more than I could chew.

Working on the principal that all platforms will inevitably die - what is the core problem? Lack of ownership.
If you don't own* the stuff (films, tv, songs) that you're consuming then you will always be held for ransom by a third party.

> * Ownership by definition is something quite clear in our heads. We "own" our shoes and normally put them on our feet, but we can do whatever the hell we like with them. Ownership in the modern world (especially digital) is akin to an indefinite rental - whilst you pay the platform owner. We have other forms of ownership too - such as that with a house. I don't "own" my home. But I own a portion of it and pay the bank back for their share of it. When I talk about ownership here I'm talking about in the truest sense of the word - it's yours and you can use it how you like.

So if ownership is the issue - then we can need a few things to break free from these platforms
1. Own media - in a format on a disk that you have
2. Have a system to share that media with devices I use

On the second point, for lack of turning to proprietary systems beyond my control, I turned to open source. Thankfully there are lots of solutions in the media space. Jellyfin is what I landed on to host my media.

The question is where I would get it from. Shockingly, this is quite difficult!
Sailing the high seas is of course an option. But I want to support the artists and creators that produced the works I enjoy. I therefore sought out a way to find said media in a good quality for download, at a price. Films wise, I couldn't find any online solution. So the only alternative was to purchase a blu-ray player and rip discs - not going to happen. For music, I only found one band I like where I could purchase and download FLAC files (for context, I checked around 30 bands).

Just as piracy was prevalent before Netflix and online streaming, I am sure that the pendulum will swing the other way as it becomes more challenging to actually obtain a copy of media.

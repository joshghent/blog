If you've spent time on certain corners of the internet, you may have stumbled across the de-google movement. The idea being to remove google services from your life.

I've been on my own privacy journey over the past year or so. And I wanted to document the efforts I've made - the stuff that was worth it, and the stuff that wasn't.

This post is entitled "De-corp your life" because whilst I was a user of Google services, like most people my digital footprint was spread across a broad range of services.

In summary I was using the following personal "tech-stack":
* Photos - Apple Photos
* Music + Podcasts - Spotify
* Movies - Netflix
* Email - Google
* File storage - Google
* Git - Github
* Todos - Todoist
* PhoneOS - iOS
* Computer OS - MacOS
* Browser - Chrome
* Maps - Google
* Notes - Apple
* LLM's - ChatGPT + Claude
* Shopping - Amazon

Whilst not all of these are Google-like evil corps, I still want to have my own selfhosted stack of applications and I was willing to trade a little bit of convenience for the privacy benefits it gave me.

## It's a journey not a destination

One of the first things I started doing was reading Michael Bazzells book - Extreme Privacy (5th edition). This is basically the bible when it comes to building a private life.
I found it a fascinating read but also quite overwhelming. To Bazzells credit, the emphasis throughout the book is to take the steps one at a time, and that it's not all or nothing - even doing something small can help.

With that in mind, I began thinking about the low hanging fruit.
For me, that was clearing up information publicly available online and my email.

Email for me was a challenging one - the classic dilemma of choice.
Eventually I settled on Proton Mail as they had a compelling offering for me as a business owner - including not only Mail but also storage, vpn and other services.
